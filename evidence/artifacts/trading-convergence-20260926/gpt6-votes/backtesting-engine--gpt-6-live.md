2. **VectorBT’s rejection contains two factual errors.** It supports multiple assets, including shared portfolio cash. Its public **v1.1.1** package also documents `vectorbt[rust]`, and its portfolio dispatcher calls `simulate_from_orders_rs`. Rust portfolio simulation is therefore not exclusively a PRO capability. Broker integration can remain a valid differentiation; “single-asset” and “Rust moved to PRO” cannot. Sources: [portfolio grouping](https://vectorbt.dev/api/portfolio/base/#grouping), [pinned README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/polakowo/vectorbt/v1.1.1/README.md), [pinned simulation dispatcher](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/polakowo/vectorbt/v1.1.1/vectorbt/portfolio/dispatch.py).

3. **Nautilus’s comparative winner rationale is circular.** Requiring alternatives to preserve the *Nautilus destination itself* makes displacement impossible by definition. Existing native receipts justify retention within their tested scope; they do not establish superiority over untested alternatives. LEAN already has an upstream IBKR integration, so even the existing co-winner deserves consideration against destination requirements. Keep Nautilus provisionally, but replace the empty challenger list with explicit comparisons and overturn conditions. Source: [LEAN IBKR integration](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/QuantConnect/Lean.Brokerages.InteractiveBrokers/master/README.md).

4. **Lumibot deserves a bounded challenger evaluation.** Its upstream documentation covers both IBKR and Alpaca, directly matching the broker requirement. The secondary-list “slow” remark is not a workload benchmark. The packet’s verification actually confirms that remark while rejecting a conflated attribution—not the performance claim through measurement. Compare completed-bar visibility, opening fills, dividend accounting, costs and broker lifecycle behavior before rejecting it. Sources: [supported brokers](https://lumibot.lumiwealth.com/brokers.html), [documented backtest timing](https://lumibot.lumiwealth.com/backtesting.alpaca.html).

5. **Two substantial candidates were omitted.** **QF-Lib** published 4.0.7 on August 24, 2026; it provides an event-driven backtester and a concrete `IBBroker` implementation. **QuantRocket’s Zipline platform** documents US-equity history and execution through both IBKR and Alpaca. These warrant screening, not automatic promotion. QuantRocket’s free tier explicitly excludes paper/live trading, so licensing is a concrete comparison constraint. Sources: [QF-Lib release](https://pypi.org/project/qf-lib/), [pinned IB implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/quarkfin/qf-lib/v4.0.7/qf_lib/brokers/ib_broker/ib_broker.py), [QuantRocket capabilities](https://www.quantrocket.com/zipline/), [pricing restrictions](https://www.quantrocket.com/pricing/).

Vibe-Trading’s README documents a specific Yahoo dividend-adjustment defect and correction, contradicting the suggestion that no relevant corporate-action discussion exists. Sources: [vn.py IB adapter](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vnpy/vnpy_ib/main/README.md), [Vibe-Trading changelog](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HKUDS/Vibe-Trading/main/README.md).

7. **RQAlpha’s licensing conclusion overstates the established blocker.** Its restrictive authorization language is real, but the same license explicitly describes permitted noncommercial personal use and nonprofit educational/research use. The proposal does not establish why this personal research stack necessarily falls into a prohibited category. Preserve the restriction and resolve the intended-use classification; remove the unconditional “regardless of technical fit” conclusion. Source: [actual license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ricequant/rqalpha/master/LICENSE).

However, its dividend implementation explicitly acknowledges inaccurate payable-date modeling: matching that oracle does not establish economically complete cash timing.
