1. **Keep `winner_status: []`, but narrow the negative conclusion.** The retained receipts support 0/1,012 development passes for the tested rules. They do not falsify continuation strategies generally: the study explicitly reports 83.8% event coverage and survivorship limitations. V3 remains an unfrozen draft. [Study evidence](blueprints/us-equities/mover-early-entry/README.md:17).

2. **The NoEdge-Bench acceptance criterion is invalid.** Its benchmark concerns a synthetic, memoryless binary-options feed. Requiring equities results to resemble its `p=0.83` example cannot establish that this pipeline is leak-free or bug-free. A large permutation p-value can also reflect an estimator’s inability to exploit a dependency; a small one cannot distinguish genuine information from leakage. Require causal timestamp checks, deliberately contaminated controls, and a dependence-aware null—not a predetermined null result. [NoEdge-Bench](https://github.com/nexusfinancial-dev/noedge-bench), [permutation-test documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.permutation_test_score.html).

3. **Exitkit/riskkit would reuse exposed validation data and weaken the original gates.** The study already publishes validation results, so that split is not “untouched” for these newly selected hypotheses. Moreover, “200 trades and positive mean” omits v1’s development significance test, validation sample requirement, BH correction, robustness conditions, and final Holm-adjusted holdout. Register a successor experiment with disclosed prior exposure and complete selection accounting. [Original acceptance criteria](blueprints/us-equities/mover-early-entry/protocol.json:103). Exitkit itself describes its example as illustrative, not a finding. [Upstream explanation](https://github.com/charlieyanhx/exitkit#works-with-backtestingpy).

4. **The TLM rejection is factually wrong.** The full paper supplies precisely what the proposal says is missing: 2,070 Russell 3000 earnings calls, dated training/test partitions, 414 test examples, and reported accuracy of 58.0% versus 50.7% for its zero-shot baseline. Its gap experiment also specifies the dataset, chronological split, and numerical results. Reclassify it as a source-reviewed, deferred candidate. Its event-conditioned sampling, information timing, costs, and small trading sample still require scrutiny; reported results do not establish tradability. [Full paper, §§5.2–5.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21625v1).

5. **The proposed orderflow-metrics port duplicates existing upstream Python code.** PyPI’s 0.40.0 already documents OFI, volume buckets/VPIN, Kyle’s lambda, tick-rule/Lee–Ready classification, depth imbalance, and book reconstruction. A higher TypeScript version does not establish a capability gap for these functions. Evaluate the supported Python package and its upstream tests before proposing a maintained local fork. [Python distribution](https://pypi.org/project/orderflow-metrics/), [upstream Python documentation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/twowaymind/orderflow-metrics/main/python/README.md).

6. **A substantial US-equity challenger was missed: relative-volume “Stocks in Play” opening-range breakout.** Zarattini–Barbon–Aziz specify selection, entries, ATR stops, risk-based sizing, a leverage cap, and end-of-day exits. QuantConnect publishes an implementation. This warrants challenging the “only full-shape system is crypto” conclusion. It also differs materially from v2’s F family: top-ranked opening-range relative volume is not the same universe as an existing ≥20% gap-up filter. Add a bounded replication candidate; neither source establishes +200% mover capture or accepted performance here. [Author paper](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/<uuid>/content), [QuantConnect implementation](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/p1).

7. **The LPPLS trial needs causal evaluation boundaries.** Selecting eventual +100%/+200% movers and fitting “pre-peak windows” can use future outcomes to choose both cases and forecast times. Define an eligible universe and rolling cutoffs available at prediction time, include false alarms, and measure executable outcomes alongside critical-time error. Also, MAX’s monthly cross-sectional return evidence does not validate intraday LPPLS reversal timing. [LPPLS definition](https://github.com/Boulder-Investment-Technologies/lppls), [original MAX paper](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~rwhitela/papers/max%20jfe.pdf).

8. **Wiseek’s accessibility and chronology are overstated.** Its public event archive covers scores ≥7; scores 1–6 are aggregate-only. The cited 13,760 is the release’s measured-filings count, not an independently verified row count for `events_score7plus.csv`. Its August 2026 release also overlaps v1’s reserved 2026 holdout period. Using those outcomes must enter the exposure ledger. Reproducing price reactions would not independently establish original score availability or reproduce the scoring model. [Publisher’s dataset specification](https://wiseek.ai/datasets/).

9. **ClusterLOB and itch-book remain conditional candidates, with narrower evidence.** ClusterLOB studies 15 large-cap stocks during 2021 regular sessions and excludes auction and halt events. That supports an order-flow research lead, not demonstrated extreme-mover or halt-resumption performance. Likewise, matching ITCH-derived outputs to Alpaca bars cannot validate order-level reconstruction. The substrate trial needs message-level invariants and the upstream differential tests, while retaining venue/feed differences. [ClusterLOB §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20349v3), [itch-book correctness checks](https://github.com/groovg/itch-book#correctness).

10. **The integrity-tool comparison misses stronger alternatives.** Compare NoEdge-Bench with `arch`’s SPA/RealityCheck and StepM, which explicitly address searching many competing models. `arch` is already catalogued locally, so this is an omitted comparison rather than necessarily another dependency. RiskLabAI also merits inspection for purged/combinatorial validation, PBO, and deflated Sharpe calculations. These address selection risk; none independently proves causal data integrity. [arch documentation](https://bashtage.github.io/arch/multiple-comparison/multiple-comparison_examples.html), [RiskLabAI](https://github.com/RiskLabAI/RiskLabAI.py).

11. **Several exclusions survive, but their reasons should remain scoped.** Crypto Pump Scanner is explicitly Bybit-perpetual infrastructure. Kelly-Criterion has stronger exclusion grounds than age alone: its documentation specifies Python 2.7 and statistically independent strategies. Kronos can remain outside this trial, but one local daily-MAE experiment does not establish general inferiority, and commit freshness is not comparative performance evidence. [Crypto source](https://github.com/stefanoviana/crypto-pump-scanner), [Kelly source](https://github.com/deltaray-io/kelly-criterion), [Kronos paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02739).

12. **Preserve the remaining literature distinctions.** The VPIN criticism supports requiring appropriate benchmarks, not rejecting every order-flow measure. The short-squeeze paper addresses conditional squeeze probability, so retain it explicitly under that hypothesis rather than treating days-to-cover mean returns as equivalent evidence. I could corroborate its publisher-indexed abstract but not inspect the full paper; the KOSPI citation also remains independently unverified.
